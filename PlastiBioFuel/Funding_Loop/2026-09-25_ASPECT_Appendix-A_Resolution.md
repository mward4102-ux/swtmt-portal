**Date resolved:** 2026-09-25

Chemical Technologies (ASPECT). DOE CMEI, Alternative Fuels and Feedstocks Office.

## The question

Modification 000002, posted 2026-09-23, states that it "Updated Appendix A."
Appendix A is the allowable feedstock table. Whether post-consumer PET survives
as an allowable feedstock is a go/no-go on a package already built against a
$2,000,000 to $20,000,000 opportunity with a concept paper due 2026-10-09.

## The answer

**Post-consumer PET is allowable, through the sorted municipal solid waste line
of Appendix A.**

Primary source: DOE's own webinar proceedings page for this NOFO, published by
the Alternative Fuels and Feedstocks Office at

    https://www.energy.gov/cmei/fuels/informational-webinar-proceedings-aspect-funding-opportunity

Josh Messner, ASPECT NOFO co-manager and Topic Area 2 lead, in the transcript
DOE publishes on that page:

> "Now, we're going to talk about acceptable feedstocks. Both topic areas of the
> NOFO have the same allowable feedstock requirements. These include
> lignocellulosic feedstocks, algae, organic wet waste, **sorted municipal solid
> waste, which include organic and plastic constituents that are going to
> landfill**, food waste, biogas from anaerobic digesters and landfills, grain
> starch, oil seed crops, construction and demolition waste, waste carbon dioxide
> produced as a byproduct from fermentation, the combustion of biomass or other
> biopower processes, or anthropomorphic CO2 from utility and industrial sources,
> and bio-intermediates, which ... refer to chemically, thermally, and physically
> stable intermediate products from another acceptable feedstock listed in the
> table above which can be upgraded into a saleable chemical.
>
> Please note that the carbon dioxide from ambient air is not allowed. ... For
> more details, you can go to the NOFO Part 1 Appendix A."

Emphasis added. Everything else in the quotation is DOE's wording.

## What is verified and what is not

Verified first hand:

- Appendix A's allowable feedstock table names sorted municipal solid waste and
  states that it includes the plastic constituents going to landfill. This is
  DOE describing the table, on a DOE page, for this NOFO.
- Modification 000002 enumerates its own Appendix A change, and that change is to
  the definition of construction and demolition waste, narrowed to waste **organic**
  materials to match the AFFO statutory authority in NOFO Part 1 Section III.G.
  The modification notice does not mention municipal solid waste or plastics.
- Modification 000002's other change moves the target-chemical test from U.S.
  production of at least 1 million metric tons to global annual markets or
  potential markets of at least 1 million metric tons. Terephthalic acid clears
  the global test comfortably.

Not verified, and the reason:

- The current verbatim text of Appendix A. The NOFO Part 1 document is a .docx.
  This container cannot retrieve it. `files.simpler.grants.gov` is refused by the
  network policy at the CONNECT stage (403), and the DOE eXCHANGE copy at
  `eere-exchange.energy.gov/FileContent.aspx` is served as a download, which the
  browser fetch tool will not return. Both paths were tried and both failed on
  2026-09-25. **Do not spend another run retrying them.**
- The webinar was held 2026-09-11, so the transcript describes Appendix A as it
  stood before Modification 000002. The inference that the sorted MSW line is
  unchanged rests on Modification 000002 enumerating its own changes and naming

## Other findings from the same DOE source that change the package

These came out of the same transcript and are more specific than anything the
grants.gov or simpler.grants.gov summaries carry.

### 1. A fuel-first framing would make the application non-responsive

DOE lists as specifically not of interest: "applications proposing primary
products that are solely used as fuels," and separately "applications proposing
ethanol as a final product."

The ASPECT concept paper must lead with terephthalic acid as a chemical product.
The RIN and LCFS value, which the company's other materials lead with, is a
liability in this application and should be left out of the concept paper or kept
well subordinate. The company name will already invite the question.

### 2. The industrial partner requirement is satisfied by the applicant

Also not of interest: "applications that do not include an industrial entity
either as the applicant or a partner." PlastiBioFuel LLC is a for-profit
industrial entity applying as prime, so this is met without a partner.

Separately, Modification 000001 moved Letters of Commitment from Stage 2 to
Stage 1. **Stage 1 is 2026-12-01, not the 2026-10-09 concept paper.** The
industrial partner Letter of Commitment is therefore not on the October critical
path. It is on the December one.

### 3. The concept paper is explicitly non-binding

DOE, in the transcript: "You are not obligated to apply after you submit your
concept paper. You are not bound to the statements made in your concept paper. It
is reasonable for project partners, locations, and other factors to change during
the application development process."

This is the practical answer to the go/no-go. A concept paper is five pages and
commits the company to nothing. The cost of submitting and being wrong about
Appendix A is five pages. The cost of not submitting is the entire opportunity,
because Stage 1 is open only to applicants whose concept paper DOE found eligible.

### 4. Topic Area 1 is the fit, and it is smaller than the headline

The $2,000,000 to $20,000,000 range spans both topic areas. Broken out:

| | Awards | Federal share | Cost share | Period |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Topic Area 1 | 0 to 7 | $2M to $10M | 20% | 2 to 4 years |
| Subtopic 1a | | up to $5M, one phase | 20% | 12 to 24 months |
| Subtopic 1b | | up to $10M, $5M per phase | 20% | phase 1: 18 to 30 months |
| Topic Area 2 | 0 to 3 | $10M to $20M | 20%/50% blended (2a), 50% (2b) | 2 to 5 years |

Topic Area 2 requires an integrated pre-pilot system processing at least half a
dry ton per day of feedstock, and 2b expects TRL 5 with all prior scale data in
hand. That is out of reach. **Topic Area 1 is the target.** Higher education is
eligible as recipient or subrecipient under Topic Area 1, so the UNT subaward
works there; under Topic Area 2 it would be subrecipient only.

Note the award counts start at zero. DOE said "anywhere from zero to seven" and
"zero to three."

### 5. Subtopic 1b carries exclusions that touch the technical approach

Specifically not of interest under Subtopic 1b:

- work on strain discovery for biochemical processes
- work on heterogeneous catalysis, catalysts in powder form, or thermocatalytic
  processes
- work on development of electrocatalytic cells
- model compounds in place of real feedstock through all budget periods

FAST-PETase is a published enzyme from the Alper group at UT Austin, Nature 2022.
Using it is not strain discovery. But any task in the workplan that reads as
enzyme or strain screening should be rewritten before a 1b submission.

Under Subtopic 1a, model compounds are permitted only through Budget Period 1.
Real post-consumer PET is required after that.

### 6. Cost share is 20% and it is real

Topic Area 1 requires a 20% minimum matching cost share. On a $5,000,000 federal
share under Subtopic 1a that is $1,250,000 of non-federal match on a $6,250,000
total project. This has to be sourced and stated. It interacts directly with the
$800,000 raise Michael opened with Rod Sanders at 310 Capital on 2026-09-21.

### 7. Submission is through DOE eXCHANGE, not grants.gov

"Concept papers, Stage 1 applications, and Stage 2 applications must be submitted
through DOE eXCHANGE. DOE will not review or consider applications submitted
through other means." A NOFO-specific concept paper template is posted on DOE
eXCHANGE. All of its content is required; use of the template itself is not.

Any how-to-submit document for ASPECT that points at grants.gov is wrong.
